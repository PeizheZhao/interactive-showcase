Flow Matching / Rectified Flow
(continuous generative modeling)

  • One of the most active research directions
  • Simple training, high-quality generation
  • Scales well with Transformer backbones

DiT / Transformer Backbones
(for diffusion and flow models)

  • Transformer as the core architecture
  • Scales to large model sizes
  • State-of-the-art image and video generation

Unified Multimodal Autoregressive
Generation

  • Native generation of text, image, video, audio
  • Unified understanding + generation + editing
  • Blurs the boundary between LLMs and
    diffusion/flow models

Fast / Few-step Generation
(consistency, distillation, etc.)

  • High-quality generation with few steps
  • Important for real-time applications
  • Active research in 2024–2026

Preference Optimization / RL

  • RLAIF, DPO, and related methods
  • Critical for real-world deployment

Key Takeaways

  • Generative modeling has evolved from EBMs → VAEs/GANs → Autoregressive
    Transformers → Diffusion → DiT → Flow Matching → Unified Multimodal Generation.
  • Different routes have different strengths and often coexist and complement each other.
  • Architecture ≠ generative objective: Transformer can be used for autoregressive, diffusion,
    or flow models (e.g., DiT uses Transformer inside diffusion/flow).
  • Diffusion, score-based models, and SDE formulations are closely related.
  • Flow matching is related to continuous flows/ODEs but distinct from classical invertible normalizing flows.
  • In 2026, the most active directions are flow matching, DiT/large transformers,
    unified multimodal generation, fast generation, preference optimization, and video/world models.
